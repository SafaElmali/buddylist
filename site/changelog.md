## v0.7.2

- **A manager's fresh session takes your messages again** — When a manager with a guest, such as Codex, filled its context window and went on in a fresh session while its workers' news came in, the fresh session never started: the window showed **Stop** with nobody at work, and a message you sent stayed queued, and **Send now** only said it was still queued. The fresh session now starts at once from the brief, with that news, and your message goes to it as soon as it has read them, or at once with **Send now**. **Start over** beside a guest no longer leaves a conversation stuck that way either.
- **Manager messages you stopped are no longer stuck** — After you stopped a worker with a guest, such as a reviewer, the messages its manager sent waited in the tray, and **Send now** on one said no agent was ready to take it. **Send now** now gives that message to the worker at once, and the others wait until you write. The tray says why they wait: “Held since you stopped: they go with your next message, or Send now.” Your next message still reads them all, in the order they came, and each only once. Your Stop still holds them, an urgent one too, and the manager is told so. The phone says the same, with **Send now**.
- **Edit a manager's message before it is read** — A message a manager sent a worker waited in the tray with **Send now** and **Cancel** only, so changing a word meant dropping it and writing it again. It now has **Edit**, as yours do: it leaves the queue unread and its words go in the message box, under a line that says whose they were, to change and send as your own message. **Put back** or Esc returns it to the queue as it was, held again if your Stop held it. The manager is told you took it, or cancelled it, so it doesn't count on the worker having read it, without being started for that. On a phone with full access, **Edit** puts its words in the box, with **Put back** under the conversation; going Back puts it back too.
- **Your messages stay in order** — When a manager's note was read as a turn ended while a message of yours waited, the message you had sent before could move below the reply, as if it had been read twice. It stays where you sent it.
- **Claude stops at the plan limit instead of using usage credits** — Once a Claude account's session or weekly limit is used up, a team or paid plan can go on in usage credits without a word, and a team member can't turn them off. Now a turn that would use them doesn't start, or stops as soon as Claude says it is using them, and the conversation says so: “Stopped at the session limit so usage credits aren’t used. Resets in 2 hr.” **Use credits for this turn** lets that one turn go on after you confirm, and **Switch account** goes on in another account. With **Automatically switch accounts at usage limits** or **Fallbacks** on, it moves to an account or service with room by itself. A manager's notes and rounds never start a stopped worker again: they wait for you. It is on by default for every account, under **Settings → Accounts**, beside **Usage credits**. On a phone, the choices need full access.
- **Allow Computer Use in an app for the conversation** — When Codex uses Computer Use, it asks before each action in an app, so a card such as “Allow Computer Use to use "Preview"?” came back on every call. The card now has a third choice, **Allow Computer Use in Preview for this conversation**, and later calls in that app go ahead without asking. It covers only that app and that Codex, and ends when its session does, after a Stop or a restart; another app still asks. A read-only Codex, such as a guest in a manager’s conversation, is offered it too, and its card says plainly that Codex could then click and type in that app; its commands and file changes stay declined. On a phone it needs full access, as **Always allow** does.
- **Background work no longer looks idle** — When an agent's turn ended with commands still running in the background, its row could look like it was doing nothing: the list beside a conversation showed no line at all, and the manager's Team tab listed it as Idle. Now its buddy wears the Background tab's arrow going round, and its line says what runs, such as “2 commands running in the background”, in the contact list, beside the conversation and in the Team tab, under **Working** there and on your phone. Point at the arrow to read the line on a narrow row or one with pull request tags. It stays Online, as you can still write to it, and the row goes back to what it said before as soon as the last command ends.

## v0.7.1 — 2026-11-07

- **Managers answer their workers’ questions** — When a worker asks you something on a card of questions and waits, its project manager now hears at once, with each question and its options. Where the brief, the sources or what you decided settle it, the manager answers, asking you first as it does for a message, and the worker goes on as if you had answered. Its card then says “Answered by” and the manager’s name, with each answer, and your phone says who answered. Otherwise the manager leaves it to you and tells you what it asks. The first answer counts, yours or the manager’s, and permissions, approvals and plans always stay yours.
- **Queued messages stay in sight** — A message you send while an agent works now waits in a tray over the message box instead of among the conversation’s lines, where the agent’s work pushed it out of sight. The tray lists what waits, oldest first, one line each with its pictures, files and, in a group conversation, who it is for, such as “to Codex”, and a click shows all of a long one. **Send now**, **Edit** and **Cancel** sit on each line: **Edit** takes it back into the message box to change, and **Cancel** now drops it unsent. Once the agent reads it, it shows in the conversation where it was read. On your phone, queued messages stay among the lines, and a line over the message box, such as “2 queued · Show”, takes you to the oldest.
- **Pick the mode a role starts in** — Each role in **Settings → Roles & teams** now has a **Permission** list with its service’s modes, such as **Never ask**, after **Default**, which keeps the mode it started in before. The role joins in it from Invite, a team, a project manager or a conversation an agent starts, and its line says so, such as “· Never asks”. **Never ask** asks you first, as the mode badge does, and so does turning Read only off under it. Read only still wins: a read-only Claude or Codex in **Never ask** runs commands that change nothing without asking, in a sandbox that can’t write, while Gemini and OpenCode stay in **Plan** and Hermes in **Ask before changing files**, the one mode of each that keeps it from changing files, and the editor says so. Where Claude Code’s sandbox can’t run, such as Linux without bubblewrap, a read-only Claude in **Never ask** or **Auto-accept file edits** doesn’t start, as nothing would keep its commands from writing, and says why and what to do.
- **No session limit for Ultra** — Verified Ultra accounts have no five-hour limit, and Claude reports none for them, so their row in **Settings → Accounts** no longer shows a Session limit that is always unknown. Their weekly limits still show, and every other plan keeps its Session limit.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-11-03

- **Choose what each phone may do** — When you pair a phone, pick **Full**, **Answer only** or **Read only** above its code, and change it any time on its row in **Settings → Remote access**. Answer only answers permissions, questions and plans and stops agents, but sends no messages, can’t always allow and approves a plan only asking before edits; Read only just looks. Your computer refuses anything else, the phone says why, and a change shows on the phone at once. Phones paired before, and a browser signed in with the password, keep full access.
- **A faster phone app that opens offline** — On a slow connection, Home now opens in about a second, with under a third of the code it needed, sent compressed; Markdown and code highlighting load while you look at Home. Once your phone has the app, opening it again asks your computer only for the page and your session until buddylist updates. With your computer asleep or Tailscale off, the app still opens and says it can’t reach it, with Retry, instead of a browser error. While agents work, the phone gets a fraction of the updates it did, only what changed in your contacts, and none while it is in the background. Each change to the phone app is now tried on a phone with a packaged build before it is merged.
- **Notifications you can check, and Decline on Android** — With notifications on, the bell on your phone offers **Send a test**, which has your computer send one at once and says whether it reached the push service, and **Turn off**, instead of turning them off in one tap. If your computer couldn’t deliver one, Home says why; when they stopped on the phone, it says so, and when turning them off couldn’t reach your computer, it says that instead of looking off or getting stuck. A phone whose browser renewed its subscription keeps getting them, and on Android, blocked notifications say where to allow them. A permission’s notification on an Android phone set to Full or Answer only now has **Decline**: it declines on your computer without opening the app. There is no Allow there, as the notification doesn’t show what would be allowed. Already answered, it says so; when it can’t reach your computer, it opens the card instead.
- **Reviewers in your workers** — Ask a project manager to “invite a Claude Opus reviewer into each worker” and it adds one to each worker’s own conversation, where you see it in the Agents tab, instead of running hidden subagents. The manager picks the service, model, effort and a role of yours; without a role the reviewer changes no files, answers after the worker’s changes and hands fixes back, within your hand-off limit. What it finds reaches the manager, and an invite you didn’t ask for asks you first. A manager starting a conversation can now give each agent it invites a model, effort level or role too.
- **PR stacks you can read at a glance** — The PR stack now reads top first, as GitHub shows a stack, down a line of linked PRs to the branch they go into. Each PR takes one row with its number, title, short branch name and an icon and colour for open, draft, merged or closed. The header gives the stack’s number, how many PRs it has and how many are merged, with a bar of its steps from the bottom up, and each PR in a GitHub stack shows its step, such as 5 of 7. A long stack opens on your conversation’s PR. A seven-PR stack now fits on one screen.
- **An easier Home on your phone** — Who waits for you comes first, with one hint at a time below it, and “Nothing needs you right now” when nobody does. A contact that asks you something stands out from one that finished, and someone at work no longer wears a red dot. With more than 10 contacts, a filter at the top finds them by name or project, archived ones included, and keeps what you typed when you come back from a conversation. Once you answer the last thing a conversation asks, “Next: planner · Add a dark theme” takes you straight to whoever waits for you next, in the order Home lists them.
- **Long conversations, start to end, on your phone** — A conversation opens with its newest 150 messages instead of all of them, a quarter of the data for a long one, and Show earlier brings the ones before, a page at a time, where you were reading, all the way to the first. Each page comes in a few parts, so the phone stays smooth while it does.
- **Nothing you type on your phone gets lost or sent twice** — A half-written message, the options you picked on a question with your own words, and feedback on a plan come back after Back, a reload or a dropped connection, and stay in their own conversation. When the connection drops just after you send, the message stays, marked “Not confirmed yet”, and is checked with your computer once it’s back, so it arrives once; an answer to a card works the same way, and you’re told to try again only when nothing left your phone. A card already answered elsewhere says so when you tap it, instead of greying out for good. On an iPhone, in Safari or the Home Screen app, the keyboard no longer pushes the message box, a question’s own answer or plan feedback off the top of the screen: they stay just above it with the header in place, a conversation read to its end stays at its end, and on its side the header shrinks to one line.
- **Clearer cards and message box on your phone** — On a permission, Decline and Allow sit side by side and “Always allow, don’t ask again” has a row of its own, so a slip of the thumb can’t grant a standing permission. A call still going says what it does now (“Wants to run …” while it waits for you), and nothing looks unfinished after Stop. While someone works, the button by the message box is Stop until you type, then Send, and the phone’s keyboard starts a new line with Return. A queued message has full-size Cancel and Interrupt and send buttons, and one that Stop drops says it wasn’t sent. A question keeps its Send button in view, your own words untick the options, and Skip is now Let it decide.
- **Long replies read well on your phone** — A wide table keeps its columns readable and scrolls sideways instead of squeezing to a letter a line, Copy sits above a code block instead of over its first line, and a picture that is on your computer shows its name instead of a broken image. Conversations no longer pan sideways, a quiet line at the end shows while someone works, and after Stop the to-do list no longer says it is still at it.
- **Your phone knows when your computer is out of reach** — After the phone sleeps, or when your computer stops answering, the phone app says so within 15 seconds instead of showing cards that can’t be answered, and a conversation says “Not reachable” instead of Online or what it was doing. A tap that doesn’t get through says why, and the card’s buttons come back. While it starts, it says it is connecting to your computer, by name, and if it can’t reach it, what to check, with Retry. The reconnecting banner no longer covers Back, and after 30 seconds it says what to check, with Retry now. Once the connection is back, the app picks up where you were instead of reloading: Home stays where you scrolled, what you were typing stays, and an open conversation shows what happened meanwhile, where you were reading, older pages included. A conversation’s Back button is easier to tap too.
- **Clearer, fairer sign-in on your phone** — A code sends itself once you’ve typed it. A wrong one stays in the box with the error right under it, and the page says how many tries are left, or whether the code was already used, ran out or is no longer in use. Wrong passwords on one phone no longer lock every phone out for 15 minutes: each counts by its own address on your tailnet, wrong codes count apart, and a new code from your computer pairs at once, even after five wrong ones. Successful sign-ins no longer count toward the limit, and Settings says which address waits, and until when, next to pairing. A phone that was signed out no longer shows a “wrong code” error before you typed anything, and the conversation you opened, from a notification too, opens once you’re in. On an iPhone, the hint and the Home Screen app’s sign-in page say it needs a new code from Pair another phone. The sign-in page uses the phone app’s colours, is easy to read by day and at night, has larger buttons and fits the smallest iPhones. Sign out now asks first, as signing in again needs a new code.
- **The phone app for every hand and eye** — Home and conversations work with a screen reader and a keyboard: Home starts at its title and a conversation at its contact’s name, going back puts you on the row you opened, and an update no longer moves you. A conversation says who wrote each message and how each step went, keeps the focus on what you just answered, and lets you reach code, tables and plans that scroll. Counts, badges, chips, fields, placeholders, code comments and a button you can’t press yet are easier to read in every theme, by day and at night, buttons are larger and easier to tap, and what has the keyboard shows a clear ring. The computer’s dot says whether your phone reaches it, and each hint’s Not now says which hint it puts away. On an iPhone or iPad, the app and its sign-in page follow Text Size and Larger Text in Settings, with the same look at the usual size; with large text, notes under a contact wrap to two lines instead of being cut short, and the header keeps its buttons whole.
- **Your phone names your computer** — With buddylist on Windows or Linux, the phone app no longer calls your computer a Mac: its sign-in page, Home, Sign out, notification and connection messages, an archived conversation, a picture from a reply and a message being checked say “your PC” or “your computer”, and the dot at the top says PC or computer. Connection messages name it as Tailscale does.
- **Remote access setup says what it sees** — On a Windows or Linux computer, Settings says PC or computer instead of Mac. Two of the same phone are told apart by when each paired, with when it signs out if unused and whether its notifications are failing. The top says which phone last reached your computer, and when. A port Tailscale Serve already uses is explained before the command, and what it printed stays one click away. A new code scrolls into view, and first-time pairing has **Copy address**. Turned on in the first moments after buddylist opened, remote access no longer refuses everything your phone asks until you restart the app.
- **Safer remote access** — Your phone is told only the theme from Settings, not your environment variables, accounts or project setup. A request that only claims to come through Tailscale Funnel no longer turns remote access off and signs your phones out: buddylist asks Tailscale first. Remote access doesn’t start with the app while Funnel is on. **Sign out all** and a new password also cancel the pairing code on screen. Keeping your computer awake is now your choice: Settings asks once after your phone pairs, and it stays off until you say yes, including if you never chose before.
- **Worker notes keep reaching a manager with guests** — A project manager with a guest, such as a read-only reviewer, now reads every note its workers send, instead of stopping at “Hand-offs paused” and piling them up as Queued. **Send now** on a queued note delivers it. Your hand-off limit still stops agents handing back and forth, counted from each note, and a manager you stopped still holds its notes until you write.
- **Stop leaves nothing looking unfinished** — After Stop, a call it cut short says “stopped” instead of “declined”, with every service, and the step a subagent was in the middle of says it was stopped when the conversation opens again, and nothing counts it as still running. A message Stop dropped before it was read says “Not sent” after a reload too, on your computer and your phone, and stays where you wrote it. When a failed turn’s recovery ends with no fallback taking over, the message box offers Send instead of Stop, and an error that ends in a full stop no longer gets a second one.
- **No more stuck “Paused” after the internet drops** — A conversation with one agent no longer ends up showing **Paused** in its Agents tab, with no way to resume it, after its turns failed. Losing your internet (an address that doesn’t resolve, a connection refused, reset or timed out) no longer counts as the service being down, so it starts no fallback: the turn says your internet connection looks down, and your next message goes through once it’s back. Conversations already stuck this way are fixed when the app starts. When a fallback does take over, the agent it replaces is paused as before.

## v0.6.12 — 2026-11-01

- **PR stacks from GitHub** — A pull request in a GitHub stack now shows every PR in that stack, bottom to top, with its position, whether it is open, a draft, merged or closed, and the current PR highlighted, even after the lower PRs merge and GitHub moves the rest onto main. A PR that GitHub moved to another base branch is no longer called standalone, and the view says whether it shows a GitHub stack or one linked by branches.
- **Manager guests hand work to workers** — An agent you invite into a manager’s conversation now answers as part of the manager. Pause the manager and ask a guest for a change, and it starts a worker under the manager instead of trying to edit the main checkout. A guest with a role, such as a reviewer, does its role’s job and hands what has to change to a worker.
- **Read-only stays read-only** — A manager, an agent in its conversation or any other read-only agent can no longer change files through its commands, whatever its mode. Codex keeps its read-only sandbox, with network access for reads such as `gh api`. Requests to run outside it are declined without an Accept prompt, and the agent is told to start a worker. Claude now runs a read-only agent’s commands in Claude Code’s sandbox, Bypass permissions included. They can’t write but can still read and use the network, and need no prompt. Where that sandbox can’t run, such as Linux without bubblewrap, Claude declines the commands it would ask about, but Bypass permissions there still runs commands unchecked. Gemini and OpenCode decline the commands they ask about.
- **Ultra accounts are called Ultra** — A Claude account on the Ultra plan (formerly Max 20x) is now called Ultra rather than Max in Settings → Accounts, the usage popover, Switch account and the notice when an account is switched. Max 5x accounts are still called Max.
- **Pinned tabs come first** — Pinning a side-pane tab moves it to the front of its row, after the tabs you pinned before it, and unpinning moves it to just after them, so pinned tabs stay together at the start. The same goes for Setup, Run and terminals below. Pins you already have come back first, in the order they had.
- **Background no longer looks like Refresh** — In the professional look, the Background button and tab now show a console with a pulse in it, for what your contact left running, instead of a reload arrow.

## v0.6.11 — 2026-10-30

- **See PR stack relationships** — See a pull request’s base, confirmed parents and dependents, with the current PR highlighted and links to open, draft, merged and closed PRs. Ambiguous or incomplete relationships are labelled without inventing a stack order.

Pins keep their order; unavailable content explains what happened, and can be unpinned. Arrow keys, Home and End move between tabs. Fallbacks explains what continues, when a backup can take over, which account it uses and when consent is needed. - **Clearer usage limits** — Session and weekly readings above 100% now say **over limit**, with the reported percentage kept visible and the bar kept within its track. Readings just below 100% no longer round up to an exhausted allowance, and unknown readings say so.

## v0.6.8 — 2026-10-29

- **buddylist on your phone** — Use buddylist from your phone, anywhere, through Tailscale, with no server of ours: your conversations stay on your Mac. A phone app of its own lists who is waiting for you first, with what each asks, then who is working, and opens a conversation to reply, allow or decline a permission, answer questions, approve a plan, send a queued message now or cancel it, and stop. Add it to your Home Screen to open it like an app. Turn it on in Settings → Remote access: set a password there, let it set up Tailscale Serve with one click (it asks first and shows what Tailscale prints), and pair a phone by scanning a QR code or typing a short code that works once. Every phone or browser signed in is listed with where it came from, and you sign each out with a click. It refuses to run while Tailscale Funnel would put it on the public internet, and can keep the Mac awake while it is on. Tap the bell to get a notification when an agent needs you, finishes or runs into a problem, and tap one to open that conversation. By default they come only while you’re away from your Mac and say only who and what kind; Settings can show what was said. On iPhone they work in buddylist on the Home Screen, on iOS 16.4 or later.
- **Reply with a picture** — Point at a picture an agent shared, such as a screenshot in its reply, one it took, or a diagram it drew, and click **Reply** in its corner; or right-click it, or open it and press **Reply** (R). It goes in the message box at its full size, under a quote that names it, such as “Claude's picture: The cart page”. Click it there to draw on it, then send it back. It works for every agent in the conversation, and for your own pictures too. One that can’t go in, such as a picture on the web or one too big to send, says why.
- **Smaller fixes** — In a narrow side pane, the box for commenting on a line of a diff keeps each button’s label whole and moves the buttons to a second line when they don’t fit, instead of breaking words and spilling past the box. The message suggested in an empty message box, and the replies under a question, are now what you would send the agent, such as “go”, rather than its own “Say go and I’ll…” written back. The Roles tab in Invite a Contact has an **Edit roles...** button that opens Settings → Roles & teams; it and Set up roles... now close the dialog first, so Settings is no longer hidden behind it. In Add a Contact, **Edit teams...** and **Set up a team...** open Settings in front of the form instead of behind it; Back or Esc brings the form back with everything you typed, and a team you make or change there is in its Team list.

## v0.6.7 — 2026-10-29

- **Project manager** — A manager over a project’s managers, for handing work between teams. Right-click the project and choose **Add a project manager...**, then tell it what to do, such as “send the coder manager’s finished jobs to the review manager”. It hands the worker’s job to that manager, with its branch, pull request and changes; the review manager starts its own reviewers, and the verdict comes back up and on to the worker’s manager, which tells the worker. It changes no files and asks you before each hand-off; once you approve one, that manager’s workers start at once. It hears only from the managers, never of every change their workers make, so its context stays small. In the contact list it leads the project, with its managers under it and their conversations under them, each folding on its own.
- **Instructions for every project** — Settings → Every project → Instructions tells the contacts of all your projects the same things, such as a skill to use or how far to take a change, in Every conversation, Create PR and Review comments, with the Presets list beside each box. Every service hears it, This computer included, before what a project’s own Instructions say, so a project can add to it or say otherwise. Only you change it: agents keep to their project’s. It takes effect from the next conversation you start.
- **Smaller fixes** — Click a picture in the message box, or press Return or Space on it, to draw on it straight away, without a separate pencil button. Messages you send while agents are working, also in a conversation with guests, now each get their own turn, in the order you sent them, instead of being answered together. A worker that wasn’t running when its manager messaged it now sends its answer back to the manager.

## v0.6.6 — 2026-10-27

- **Continue workers with another service** — When workers stop at a usage limit, right-click their manager, or open its Team menu, and choose **Continue workers with...**. Pick the conversations, then a service, model and effort. Each worker keeps its folder, branch, changes and history, its previous agent is paused, and its permissions stay the same or get narrower. You see how each one went, and can retry only the ones that failed.
- **Draw on a picture before you send it** — Click the pencil on a picture in the message box, or Draw with it open, to mark what matters with a pen, a box, an arrow or a few words, in red or another ink. Undo and Redo (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z) go a step at a time. Done puts the drawing on the picture at its full size, so the agent sees exactly what you marked; Cancel or Esc leaves it as it was. Screenshots taken in the Browser tab get Redo and Return for Add to message too.
